## Overall Impression

The craft floor here is genuinely high — the type system is real infrastructure, the lightbox is a correctly hand-rolled dialog, the evidence discipline never cheats. The problem is distribution: every original idea lives on `/music`, `/lens`, or in the index handoff, and the home page below the fold is a résumé in a nice typeface. The single biggest opportunity is moving one artifact from each discipline onto `/`.

## What's Working

1. **The type and colour system is infrastructure, not decoration.** Four page titles that each had their own value *and* their own responsive behaviour collapsed into one fluid tier scale; nine bespoke prose sizes became four named roles; tracking is size-specific and retuned for the actual face. The accent is split by role — `#2563eb` (3.87:1) for shapes, `#4478ee` (4.93:1) for words — and the split is honoured at callsites.
2. **The lightbox is the most complete interaction on the site.** Verified end to end: `role=dialog`, `aria-modal`, body scroll locked, focus to close on open, arrows navigate, Escape closes, focus returns to the triggering thumbnail, scroll position preserved. Nine tab stops cycled correctly with `inDialog=true` at every stop.
3. **Evidence discipline matches the product contract exactly.** 33 skills as a flat inventory with no bars or tiers; "Private" as an honest labelled status rather than a dead link; real screenshots including the actual award cheque; no testimonials, logos, or invented metrics anywhere.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The expanded sidebar occludes 124px of every page, on hover, on every route.**
`Sidebar.tsx:92` animates the fixed `<aside>` from 52px to 176px on hover; `AppShell.tsx:124` pads the content column by a hardcoded `lg:pl-[52px]` that never changes, and the rail is opaque. Measured on `/lens`: aside 52→176px, `h1` left constant at 100px in every state — the title rendered as "ns", a caption as "alls in Yosemite National Park", the transport title as "nsion". Also reproduces right after clicking a nav link with the pointer resting on the rail, contradicting the comment at `Sidebar.tsx:102-108`. *Why it matters:* it fires on the most ordinary gesture on the site — moving the mouse toward the navigation — and it is the only defect that makes the site look broken rather than opinionated. *Fix:* drive the content offset from the same `expanded` state, or make the expansion an acknowledged overlay, or simplest — ship a permanently labelled 176px rail, which also resolves the next issue. → `/impeccable adapt`

**[P1] Primary navigation has no focus-visible style and never shows labels to a keyboard user.**
`SidebarItem`'s `<Link>` (`Sidebar.tsx:30-34`) and the four `GridIndex` cells (`GridIndex.tsx:155-160`) declare no focus treatment; the rail expands on `onHoverStart` only. All nine nav stops compute `outline: 1px rgb(0,95,204)` — Chrome's UA default, ≈2.4:1 on `#080808` — not the `ring-2 ring-accent ring-offset-4` the site uses in four other places. With `aside a` focused: width 52px, label opacity 0. *Why it matters:* `PRODUCT.md` names explicit focus-visible styles a binding commitment, and for the two most important controls it is false — on a site whose hero copy claims "a focus on creating accessible user experiences". *Fix:* apply the existing `ICON_LINK_CLASS` focus treatment to both, and expand the rail on focus-within. → `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] The résumé — the primary recruiter conversion artifact — exists in exactly one place, in the footer.**
Grep across `app/` for `resume` returns one hit: `Footer.tsx:7`, a 14px link in a four-link row, 3,146px down on desktop and 4,377px on mobile. Meanwhile `Hero.tsx:132` states "Seeking: Software engineering internships" in the first viewport with no adjacent action, and `/reach` offers no résumé at all. *Why it matters:* `PRODUCT.md` makes `resume.pdf` a first-class artifact and defines success as leaving with a way to make contact. The hero announces availability and offers nothing to act on. *Fix:* put a bordered secondary download in the hero status card directly under the "Seeking" line, and a second on `/reach` beside the email address. → `/impeccable onboard`

**[P2] The contact form claims to send, then destroys the message it did not send.**
`ContactForm.tsx:244` reads `{sending ? 'Sending' : 'Send'}`; lines 75-84 add an artificial 420ms delay with a sweeping accent rule before `window.location.href = mailto:`; lines 115-131 replace the form with a confirmation whose copy retracts the claim; line 87-94 `reset()` clears the draft. The *clipboard-failure* branch (150-163) renders the full draft in a selectable textarea — the success path shows less than the failure path. *Why it matters:* the product forbids claiming delivery because delivery cannot be detected. The confirmation copy honours that; the button, the loading label and the check icon — everything the user sees first — do not. Concrete failure: a recruiter on a locked-down laptop with no mail handler sees "Sending", sees a checkmark, nothing opens, and their three paragraphs are gone. *Fix:* label the button "Open in email app" with a one-line hint, drop the fake delay, always render the draft, replace `Check` with a neutral mail glyph, and give "Email directly" visual primacy. → `/impeccable clarify`

**[P2] Mobile drops the wrong information on `/music` and the entire human layer on `/reach`.**
`TrackRow.tsx:114` hides BPM + duration below `lg` while keeping a truncated description ("Distorted and cloudy synths paired with cat…"); tempo survives in the row's `aria-label`, so a screen-reader user on mobile receives more than a sighted one. `reach/page.tsx:84` makes the four personal photos `hidden lg:block`, leaving a 952px mobile page of heading, one line, three inputs and a button. *Why it matters:* both times the smaller screen loses the thing that is short, structured and specific, and keeps the thing that is long and generic — and `/reach` mobile is the likeliest arrival from a LinkedIn or résumé link. *Fix:* stack `146 BPM · 2:11` under the title on mobile; render the montage below the form as a 4-across strip. → `/impeccable adapt`

**[P3] The home page's highest-value pixel is its most generic element, and the range claim has no artifact.**
`RoleCycle.tsx:6-17` rotates six roles on a 4000ms hold — three are non-engineering, so a recruiter giving the page 8 seconds may read only "Photographer" or "Audio Engineer". Below the index band, `HomeContent.tsx:26-48` is entirely `ExperienceList`: no beat, no photograph, no project screenshot appears on `/`. *Why it matters:* `PRODUCT.md` makes range a structural commitment and requires every claim to have an artifact. On home the claim has two nav labels. *Fix:* one strip below Experience with a playable beat row, two or three photographs, and the featured project; and either weight the cycler so an engineering role leads or replace it with a static line. → `/impeccable bolder`

## Persona Red Flags

**Recruiter / hiring manager (~30s, from a résumé link on a work laptop — the primary audience):** ~2.5s opaque loader with no progress or skip, replayed on every hard load including returning from `resume.pdf`. The only résumé link is 3,146px down at 14px. The first word they read has a ~50% chance of being "Photographer", "Audio Engineer" or "Music Producer". Five unlabeled icons to get from `/builds` to contact, and hovering to find out covers the page. `/builds` is 7,169px with no filter or tech index, so checking "has he shipped React" means reading eleven descriptions. Clicking Send on a machine with no mail handler eats their message.

**Engineer / peer reading it as a work sample:** they will tab — nine nav stops with a 1px `#005fcc` UA ring on `#080808` (≈2.4:1) and permanently invisible labels, on a site claiming accessibility focus in its hero copy. They will notice within ten seconds that the rail width and the content padding are not driven by the same value. The repo link — the one thing they want — is a 19px `#7d7d7d` glyph. Untreated full-bleed white screenshots against `#080808` read as the one place nobody made a decision.

**Cold arrival at `/music` from a shared beat link:** the page opens with the word "Music" and a filter row; Snehil's name appears nowhere except the footer copyright. 22 undifferentiated alphabetical rows, no featured or recent track, no ordering signal. On mobile they get a name and half a sentence. The six-option filter row has a 15.92px-wide default target. The analyser bars and tempo-breathing transport — the reason to be here — are invisible until a click on a 12px triangle.

## Questions to Consider

- The three unforgeable ideas here took real invention. Why did the most original thinking go to the two pages a recruiter may never open, and the most borrowed thinking to the one they always see?
- Navigation is the only place Builds, Music and Lens carry equal weight. Below the index band, `/` is 100% engineering résumé. Is the range claim structural, or is it nav labels with a résumé underneath?
- Someone understood the mailto constraint deeply enough to write the most careful copy on the site, then decorated it with the exact claim it forbids. What made the button feel exempt from the rule the paragraph obeys?
- The email address sits one line above a three-field form that produces a mailto the address alone would produce in one click. Is the form worth three required fields, a fake 420ms state, and the risk of eating someone's message?
- Two `hidden` classes decide what mobile loses, and both times it loses the short, structured, specific thing. What principle is choosing what to cut — or is it just whichever column was easiest to hide?
