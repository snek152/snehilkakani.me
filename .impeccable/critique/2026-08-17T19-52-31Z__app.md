## Design Specificity and anti-vibecode verdict

The website is authored, not template-derived. Its self-hosted type pair, peculiar per-route signals, beat-timed transport, justified photography grid, real gesture physics, factual artifacts, and documented anti-references are hard evidence of human iteration.

The static detector returned `[]` for `app`. This is a clean source-pattern result, not a complete DOM-level detector pass: its Puppeteer browser engine and visual overlay could not run because the skill environment does not have Puppeteer. Manual browser inspection covered all five routes and both desktop/mobile home.

Gradient verdict: the gradients are not broadly AI-coded. They are confined to thin seams, route signals, and 1px project/rule lines; there are no gradient headings, button fills, cards, blobs, glass surfaces, or rainbow palettes. The two cues that do lean toward contemporary AI-portfolio vocabulary are the global cursor-follow radial glow and the ProjectCard `blur-3xl` proximity bloom. They are restrained, but they are also the only effects that threaten the otherwise disciplined system.

Creativity/authorship: **7/10**. Recruiter-confidence: **6/10**. The evidence is unusually strong; its first-fold hierarchy and project taxonomy fail to foreground that evidence quickly enough.

## What works

1. **Artifact-backed proof.** The $10K award photograph, GU-Net accuracy with denominators, named publication venue, Lindy role, original beats, and original photography make claims credible.
2. **System restraint.** Three greys, a role-split blue, real contrast math, hairlines instead of card elevation, and self-hosted type create a recognizably authored instrument-panel language.
3. **Product-specific interaction.** Route-specific SVG signals, transport behavior tied to track tempo, image geometry, and lightbox drag physics feel built for this portfolio rather than borrowed from a landing-page kit.

## Priority issues

### P0 — The recruiter conversion path has no hierarchy
**Evidence:** Home presents Résumé, GitHub, LinkedIn, and Email as visually equivalent controls. At 390px, the first viewport has no action at all; the action row sits below the portrait/status block.

**Impact:** The success condition is a recruiter quickly understanding the candidate and reaching the résumé/contact route. The page makes them scroll before offering any action, then ranks the résumé equally with secondary destinations.

**Recommendation:** Establish Résumé as the single primary action and make an action available in the first mobile viewport. Suggested command: `/impeccable layout`.

### P1 — The hero weakens the central range claim
**Evidence:** The role cycler takes 24 seconds to show six titles that repeat three disciplines: Software/AI/Full-Stack and Music/Audio. The paragraph calls music and photography “in my free time.”

**Impact:** A recruiter can first see “Photographer” and infer creative generalist rather than engineering candidate. The cycling/scramble device also reads more generically generated than the site's otherwise specific work.

**Recommendation:** Reduce to one stable engineering-forward line with concise, non-hierarchical evidence of music and photography; surface one genuine artifact per discipline on Home. Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify` followed by `/impeccable layout`.

### P1 — The best proof is buried in long, unstructured routes
**Evidence:** The award is prose on the second page. Builds is eleven alternating rows with no filter or top-project entry point; Lens is 36 images without series framing; Home ends on the least differentiating experience entry.

**Impact:** A recruiter cannot efficiently locate infrastructure/AI evidence. Photography can read as an unedited camera roll, undermining the stated peer-discipline positioning.

**Recommendation:** Promote the award, prioritize or facet Builds, and curate Lens into named editorial groups. Suggested command: `/impeccable shape`.

### P2 — Skills taxonomy creates an avoidable credibility leak
**Evidence:** Cursor, Copilot, Claude, and Gemini are listed as AI/ML alongside PyTorch/LangGraph; Java and C are filed as Tools.

**Impact:** Immediately after the most technical project evidence, this reads as unprincipled inventory padding to an engineering reviewer.

**Recommendation:** Separate languages, ML frameworks, and AI tooling; remove or reframe consumer products. Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`.

### P2 — Two glow treatments are the only credible “AI gradient” concern
**Evidence:** `CursorGlow` uses an input-driven radial accent gradient. `ProjectCard.tsx` adds a proximity-driven `bg-accent blur-3xl` layer at up to 6% opacity.

**Impact:** Neither is a generic gradient system, but together they add a familiar futuristic portfolio cue that competes with the more original hairline language. The ProjectCard treatment is also undocumented in DESIGN.md.

**Recommendation:** Choose whether direct cursor feedback is essential; remove the ProjectCard bloom first if distilling. If retained, document the treatment and its purpose. Suggested command: `/impeccable distill`.

### P3 — Minor execution debt
- Mobile secondary controls are ~37px tall: AA-compliant, but under the 44px AAA comfort target.
- The Lens `h1` may contain duplicate accessible/visual heading text; verify and dedupe if confirmed.
- RoleCycle combines the MD size token with SM tracking, contrary to the documented tier pairing.
- Production performance has not been profiled; technical score is code-and-browser evidence, not a Lighthouse result.

## Persona red flags

- **Recruiter, 30-second scan:** Sees a rotating “Photographer” label and four equal action controls before the strongest engineering evidence. The résumé has no priority.
- **Engineering hiring manager:** Reaches credible GU-Net metrics, then sees a muddled skills taxonomy; it risks downgrading confidence in the preceding evidence.
- **Mobile referral visitor:** Has to scroll beyond the first fold to find a link or action on Home.
